Fun ways to pick a winner for raffles, classes and parties

By Vincent Carl Benlot · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer

The most fun ways to pick a winner turn the draw into something people watch: a spinning wheel, a ball race, a horse race or a last-one-standing battle on screen, or classics like names in a hat and raffle tickets. Pick based on your group size, whether you have a screen, and whether people need proof the draw was fair.

On-screen ways to pick a winner

These all work on a projector, a TV or a shared screen on a video call. In SpinDraw they all use the same fair draw behind the scenes. The game only changes how the winner is revealed.

1. Spin the wheel

The classic. Every name gets a slice, the wheel spins, and the pointer lands on the winner. It’s quick, everyone understands it straight away, and it handles thousands of names. It suits big lists and quick decisions.

2. Ball race

Every name becomes a ball that drops through pegs, spinning bars and bumpers, and the first one across the finish line wins. A live board shows who’s leading, so people cheer for their ball the whole way down.

3. Horse race

Each person gets a pixel horse and jockey, and the lead changes as they gallop to the finish. It’s a crowd-pleaser at office parties. With the Christmas theme on, the horses turn into reindeer.

4. Pixel battle

Names become little pixel fighters who dodge, block and knock each other out while a circle closes in. The last one standing wins. Expect a lot of noise from whoever’s watching.

Classic offline ways to pick a winner

5. Names in a hat

Write every name on the same size paper, fold them the same way and give the hat a good shake. It’s simple and fun in a room, but it doesn’t work for anyone on a call, and nobody can check it afterwards.

6. Raffle tickets

Hand out numbered tickets from a double roll and draw the matching half from a bowl. It’s great for events with walk-in guests. If people pay for tickets, check your local rules first, because paid raffles often need a licence.

7. Playing cards

For small groups, deal one card each and draw a matching card from a second deck. Whoever holds the match wins.

8. Dice

Fine for six people or fewer. Give each person a number and roll. Roll again on a tie.

How to choose the right method

  • Over 100 people? Use the wheel, which handles lists of any size.
  • Remote or hybrid group? Pick something on screen so everyone sees the same draw.
  • Prize worth arguing about? Choose a method people can check afterwards, not just one they watched.
  • Just want a laugh? Go for a race or a battle. Watching a friend’s horse lose the lead never gets old.

Whatever you pick, a quiz or a game of skill isn’t a random draw. If the prize should be down to luck, use one of the methods above.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most fun way to pick a winner?

A draw people can watch build up, like a ball race or horse race where the lead changes, is usually more fun than an instant result. A spinning wheel is the quickest option that still has some suspense.

What is the fairest way to pick a winner?

Use a random draw that everyone can watch and that can be checked afterwards. Locking the list before the draw and sharing a verify link is fairer than a screenshot, because it shows the result came from the list.

How do you pick a winner from a large group?

For more than 100 names, use a spinning wheel, which handles thousands of entries. The race and battle games suit groups of up to 100.

Can I pick more than one winner at once?

Yes. In SpinDraw’s ball race, horse race and pixel battle, you can set how many winners you want, up to 50.
