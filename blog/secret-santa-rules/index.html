Secret Santa rules: a simple guide for work, family and friends

By Vincent Carl Benlot · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer

The core Secret Santa rules are: agree a spending limit, draw names so nobody gets themselves, set a date for the swap, share wishlists, keep your person secret until the reveal, and keep gifts suitable for everyone in the group. Agree these up front and the whole thing runs itself.

What is Secret Santa?

Secret Santa is a gift exchange where everyone draws one person’s name and buys a present for them, without saying who they drew. It goes by plenty of names: Kris Kringle in Ireland and Australia, Wichteln in Germany, Amigo Secreto across Latin America and Monito-Monita in the Philippines. Wikipedia has a good overview if you’re curious about the history.

The six Secret Santa rules to agree first

  1. Set a spending limit. Pick one number and stick to it, so nobody feels outdone or out of pocket. For work groups, keep it low enough that it’s easy for everyone.
  2. Draw names fairly. Nobody should get themselves, and nobody should be able to fix who gets whom. More on this below.
  3. Set the dates. One deadline to join, one date for the draw, and one date for the swap.
  4. Share wishlists, or at least a hint. Three ideas from each person saves a lot of guessing and a lot of bath sets.
  5. Keep it secret. Don’t tell your person, and don’t trade names behind the organiser’s back.
  6. Keep gifts suitable. At work especially, skip anything that could embarrass someone. If it’s a gag gift, it should be funny for the person opening it too.

How do you draw names for Secret Santa fairly?

Names in a hat has two problems: someone eventually draws their own name, and the last few people can work out who has whom. A neater method is a gift chain. Put everyone in a random order, then each person buys for the next person in the order, and the last person buys for the first. Because it’s one big loop, nobody can get themselves.

You can make that random order with a spinning wheel. Spin until the wheel is empty and the draw order is your chain. Our Secret Santa name picker guide shows exactly how, including how to keep the pairs private.

One honest limit: SpinDraw doesn’t do exclusion rules, like keeping partners apart, and it doesn’t email people their match. If you need those, a dedicated Secret Santa app is the better choice.

Extra Secret Santa rules for the office

  • Make it optional. Some people can’t or don’t want to join. That should be fine, with no questions asked.
  • Keep the budget low and fixed. It protects junior staff from feeling pressure to spend.
  • Agree what’s off limits. Alcohol, joke gifts about appearance and anything too personal are common no-gos at work.
  • Plan for remote colleagues. Give an earlier deadline so gifts arrive by post in time, or allow e-gift cards.

Fun ways to do the reveal

The reveal is half the fun. Have everyone guess their Secret Santa before they’re told. Or open gifts one at a time, in an order you draw at random, so it doesn’t drag. On a video call, draw the opening order live on screen so everyone sees who’s next.

Secret Santa vs white elephant: what’s the difference?

In Secret Santa, you buy for one specific person. In a white elephant gift exchange, also called a Yankee swap, everyone brings one gift for the pile, then people take turns choosing or stealing. White elephant needs no drawing of names, just a fair turn order, which you can draw the same way.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good Secret Santa budget?

Pick an amount everyone in the group can comfortably afford and keep it fixed. For work groups, a low limit is kinder, because it stops anyone feeling pressure to spend more than they want to.

How do you make sure nobody draws their own name in Secret Santa?

Use a gift chain: put everyone in a random order, each person buys for the next one, and the last person buys for the first. It’s one big loop, so nobody can get themselves.

Can you do Secret Santa remotely?

Yes. Draw names online, set an earlier deadline so posted gifts arrive in time, or allow e-gift cards, then do the reveal on a video call.

Is Secret Santa the same as Kris Kringle?

Yes. Kris Kringle, Wichteln, Amigo Secreto and Monito-Monita are local names for the same idea: drawing one person’s name and buying them a gift in secret.
