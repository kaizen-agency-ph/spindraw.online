How to pick a random winner for a giveaway, and prove it was fair

By Vincent Carl Benlot · 7 November 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

To pick a random giveaway winner, put every valid entry into one list, remove duplicates, lock the list, then draw the winner with a random tool your audience can watch. Give the winner a set time to claim, redraw if they don’t, and share proof of the draw so nobody can say it was rigged.

Step by step: picking a giveaway winner

  1. Close entries at the time you announced. Late entries are the first thing people complain about.
  2. Gather every entry into one list. Comments, form responses or a spreadsheet, one name per line.
  3. Remove duplicates and invalid entries. One person, one entry, unless your rules said otherwise.
  4. Lock the list. Freeze it before the draw so nobody can be quietly added or removed.
  5. Draw live. On stream, on a screen share or recorded, so people see the winner come out.
  6. Give the winner a claim window. Say how long they have to reply, and redraw if they don’t.
  7. Share the proof. Post something people can check, not just a screenshot.

How do you get entries out of comments?

Most giveaways run in the comments. The fiddly part is turning a messy comment thread into a clean list of names. In SpinDraw you copy the comments from Facebook, YouTube or TikTok, paste them in, and choose the copied-comments format. The likes, timestamps and “Reply” links are stripped out, and you can count only comments that contain your keyword, like #JOIN. If you collected entries with Google Forms, import the sheet or a CSV instead. Our Facebook Live raffle guide covers the comment method in detail.

Should duplicate entries count?

Usually not. If one person comments ten times, keeping all ten gives them ten times the chance, which isn’t what most giveaways intend. Turn on “one entry per person” before you draw.

If you do want bonus entries, for example for people who shared the post, say so in the rules first. In SpinDraw you can write Maria Santos x3 to give someone three entries, and the verify page lists every weight, so it stays out in the open.

Why “random” isn’t enough

Almost any picker is random enough. The real question your audience has is different: how do they know you didn’t spin again until a friend won, or quietly drop a name? A screenshot can’t answer that, because it only shows the attempt you chose to post.

The fix is to commit before you draw. SpinDraw locks your list and a secret random seed behind a code that’s shown on screen before anything moves. After the draw it reveals the seed, so anyone can re-run the draw and get the same winner. Cryptographers call this a commitment scheme. For extra trust, ask a viewer for a random word after you lock, and mix it in, so even you can’t steer the result.

What if the winner doesn’t reply?

Decide this before the giveaway and put it in the rules. A common approach is a fixed claim window, from a few minutes on a live stream to a few days for a post. If the winner misses it, draw again. In SpinDraw the claim timer counts down for you, and pressing R logs a no-show and redraws that prize, so the record shows exactly what happened.

Check the platform’s rules

Social platforms have their own promotion rules. Meta’s Pages, Groups and Events policies cover promotions on Facebook, and Instagram has its own promotion guidelines. Read them before you launch, especially if your entry rules involve tagging or sharing. And if entries are tied to buying something, the giveaway may count as a sales promotion where you live, which can need a permit.

Protect your winner from scammers

Giveaways attract fake accounts that copy your name and message entrants claiming they’ve won, then ask for a “delivery fee”. Tell your audience up front how you’ll contact the real winner, and that you’ll never ask them to pay anything to claim a prize.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fairest way to pick a giveaway winner?

Lock the entry list before the draw, draw with a random tool everyone can watch, and share proof afterwards that anyone can check. Asking a viewer for a random word to mix in makes it hard for even the host to steer the result.

How do I pick a random winner from comments?

Copy the comments, paste them into a comment picker that strips likes and timestamps, filter by your entry keyword and remove duplicates. Then draw from the clean list.

Should I let people enter more than once?

Only if your rules say so. Most giveaways count one entry per person. If you give bonus entries, announce them in advance and keep them visible.

What should I do if the winner never responds?

Set a claim window in your rules before the giveaway. If the winner misses it, log them as a no-show and draw again for that prize.
